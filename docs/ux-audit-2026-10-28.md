# Thermals UX audit, 2026-10-28

Original audit method: design review (detector not applicable, native platform). The owner subsequently authorized all 13 fixes; implementation results and verification limits are recorded below.

## Judgment

Thermals has a distinctive, coherent visual identity and a strong temperature-first premise. Keep the split-flap board. The main UX weakness is the distance between showing measurements and helping someone interpret them. The interface makes utilization, heat and system pressure look more interchangeable than they are, and offers a dense inventory where the product promise calls for a quick explanation.

The intended loop is: notice heat, judge urgency, inspect likely contributors, return to work. The menu-bar concept and hero reading support the first step well. The second and third steps need clearer semantics and quicker attribution. These are expert-review findings, not measured user failure rates.

## Reviewed journey

1. **Notice the menu-bar reading.** Temperature, load or both are supported. The menu-bar image has a descriptive accessibility label when readings exist. Pressure propagation has important edge cases, described in UX-07. The actual menu-bar surface was inspected in source only.
2. **Open HEAT and judge urgency.** The 47-degree and 93-degree snapshots give the hero strong visual priority. Power, load and pressure provide useful context. The sensor inventory explains where readings came from, but not which current app is contributing.
3. **Open CPU to investigate.** Total load, per-core detail and ranked processes are present. The different percentage scales are unexplained. Entering the board clears prior process results and waits for a fresh delta.
4. **Open RAM for memory context.** Used memory, swap and OS pressure are useful. Neutral breakdown rows avoid making every allocation a warning. Heat words applied to app footprint and used percentage are harder to interpret; physical capacity is not stated alongside the percentage headline.
5. **Adjust preferences.** Five persistent groups have simple immediate choices. They take roughly 180 vertical points including row spacing on every visit, even when the task is just checking heat. All three board tabs plus the twelve preference options create 15 visible choice buttons, but each local group is small. The problem is competing task priorities rather than an excessively large individual menu.
6. **Encounter missing data or recover.** The unavailable HEAT/RAM renders are visually honest about absent numbers, but still imply ongoing collection. The login-failure row is better: it names the failure and provides a Settings route. Pending approval lacks an equivalent explanation.
7. **Install an update.** The update row is discoverable when present and hands off to Sparkle rather than inventing a custom installer. The extra row increases intrinsic height. The installation flow itself was not exercised.

## What to preserve

- The strong hero reading, aligned columns and coherent material vocabulary make the app identifiable and visually ordered.
- Fixed sensor/core ordering provides a repeatable scan path. Ranked processes are appropriate for identifying current contributors; smoothing and motion reduction are thoughtful.
- Color is reinforced by words on sensor/process/app rows. Most rows have explicit combined accessibility labels; hidden boards and blank rows are excluded from accessibility. Selected controls expose selected traits.
- Cmd-1/2/3 and Cmd-Q exist in source. Flap and ranking animations honor Reduce Motion. Actual focus behavior still needs a live check.
- Sampling expensive process data only when requested is a sound energy-conscious constraint, even though entry latency needs attention.
- Celsius/Fahrenheit and menu-bar modes are straightforward. Missing values are not replaced with invented measurements. Power remains neutral because a shared watt threshold would be misleading across chips.
- The observed login failure has a recovery action, and updates use the native Sparkle flow.

The always-dark board is an explicit design decision, not an accidental failure to implement light mode. The review does not recommend replacing the visual world with a generic macOS statistics list.

## Priority synthesis

1. **Make the monitoring contract trustworthy.** Address stale readings, pressure propagation and misleading health/utilization words. The user needs to know what is measured, how recent it is, and whether the OS reports pressure.
2. **Make diagnosis fast and understandable.** Explain CPU denominators, obtain a prompt second process sample, and offer a direct path from HEAT to current CPU contributors. Do not claim current activity proves the cause of an earlier spike.
3. **Make the transient popover fit its job.** Keep a bounded summary, history and diagnosis path prominent; place rarely changed preferences behind a Settings control. Preserve stable positions within the available height instead of relying on an unbounded tallest-board layout.
4. **Improve recovery and readability.** Distinguish unsupported, waiting, empty, failed and stale states; make pending OS approval legible; preserve full names and numeric magnitude; strengthen small warning text.
5. **Verify adversarial states.** Include mixed pressure/utilization, failed reads, long names, larger core counts, constrained screens and live accessibility. Existing all-cool/all-hot snapshots cannot prove these contracts.

## Persona and emotional journey

**Developer between builds:** the large temperature and direct menu-bar access are useful. Entering CPU can consume much of a brief check while it samples, and the process that caused a short spike may already be gone. Reassurance should come from a clear pressure state and recent evidence, not only a cool-colored number.

**New Mac power user:** the interface is approachable at the board-navigation level, but BURN beside a process, NOMINAL pressure, and 298% CPU demand background knowledge. Inline definitions and explicit units have more value than a tutorial wizard.

**User with reduced vision or using a keyboard/screen reader:** row-level accessibility labels and Reduce Motion are good foundations. Small condensed type, hinge lines, limited resizing, weak critical-text contrast and trend information reduced to extrema need attention. Keyboard success is unknown, not assumed broken.

The emotional high point is immediate recognition of the live number and the mechanical board's character. The low points are an alarming label without a clear implication and an unavailable state that still promises collection. The desired ending of a check is confidence about current conditions and the next useful step.

## Measurements and accessibility limits

Current normal renders are 400 by 855 points; the update and login-error variants are 400 by 891 points; unavailable HEAT/RAM are 400 by 798 points. Before the concurrent heat-alert row, those heights were 819, 855 and 762 points respectively. These dimensions come from PNG sizes divided by the test renderer's explicit scale of 2. A standard 800-point-high logical desktop cannot contain an 855-point intrinsic panel plus menu-bar margins without some host accommodation. Actual macOS accommodation was not observed.

Token contrast uses the standard sRGB relative-luminance calculation. Burn red versus the top/bottom leaf base colors measures 3.62:1 and 4.02:1; neutral labels versus the frame measure 5.94:1. Actual leaves use gradients, so test the rendered range before making conformance claims. The small condensed warning text warrants a 4.5:1 normal-text target; this is an accessibility benchmark, not a blanket assertion that this native app fails WCAG. See [W3C contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

Apple treats memory pressure as an indicator of how efficiently memory serves processing needs and derives it from several inputs, not simply the percent occupied. That supports making pressure interpretation more prominent than alarming memory-use words. See [Apple's Activity Monitor memory documentation](https://support.apple.com/guide/activity-monitor/view-memory-usage-actmntr1004/mac).

## Tracked findings

### UX-01: Distinguish thermal health from resource utilization in warning labels

P1. [GitHub #17](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/17).

**Evidence:** HeatLevel applies COOL/WARM/HOT/BURN to temperatures, CPU percentages and memory shares. Fan rows also use the load heat scale. A process at 85% of one core is BURN; memory at 90% is BURN even with normal OS memory pressure. HeatBoard applies CPU-oriented temperature anchors to battery and SSD sensors too. Fresh cpu-hot and ram-cool renders show the shared vocabulary.

**Impact:** Users cannot reliably distinguish useful work, high utilization and a condition requiring attention. BURN can imply physical overheating for a memory allocation or CPU process.

**Proposed outcome:** Retain the board and gradient identity, but define metric-specific words and explain OS pressure separately. Make no component safety claims from generic CPU thresholds. Add contextual explanations for pressure, memory categories and what each status means.

**Acceptance:** A busy process with nominal thermal state and high memory usage with normal pressure can be understood as utilization without implying overheating. Real serious/critical OS conditions remain prominent. Check mixed states, not only all-cool and all-hot.

**Source:** Thermals/Sensors/HeatBand.swift; Thermals/Views/HeatBoard.swift; CPUBoard.swift; RAMBoard.swift

### UX-02: Explain whole-system versus per-core CPU percentages on the CPU board

P1. [GitHub #18](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/18).

**Evidence:** CPUBoard shows a whole-machine headline and per-core process percentages under %CPU, without explaining their different denominators. The fresh hot render shows 96% overall, Xcode 298%, and KERNEL + OTHERS 403%. ProcessSampler explicitly uses one-core percent. The remainder subtracts every sampled process, not merely the five displayed, and uses unsmoothed values while ranked rows are smoothed.

**Impact:** The board looks numerically contradictory and its visible rows cannot be treated as an additive breakdown of the headline.

**Proposed outcome:** Label the whole-system and one-core scales, including why process values can exceed 100%. Describe KERNEL + OTHERS as an estimate of unmeasured work, not the sum of all omitted rows. Do not imply that smoothed top-five values reconcile exactly.

**Acceptance:** A user can explain how a 298% process coexists with a 96% headline without reading README. Accessible labels convey the denominator and estimated remainder semantics.

**Source:** Thermals/Views/CPUBoard.swift; Thermals/Monitor.swift:hiddenLoad; Thermals/Sensors/ProcessSampler.swift

### UX-03: Track successful sample freshness and stop presenting stale history as current

P1. [GitHub #19](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/19).

**Evidence:** Monitor.refresh updates updatedAt before CPU sampling, retains cpuLoad/coreLoads if CPULoad.sample fails, and then appends the retained cpuLoad with a new timestamp. History pruning occurs only on append. LampMeter.columnPeaks anchors time to the last reading and fills empty slices after the first reading with the prior value. These are source-confirmed paths, not injected live failures.

**Impact:** An advancing UPDATED footer and filled history can falsely reassure a user that old values were just measured. Missing temperature or memory data can leave old history labelled LAST 3 MIN indefinitely.

**Proposed outcome:** Track freshness per successful source, age/prune against wall time, clear or mark stale current readings, and distinguish unobserved long gaps from normal interpolation at the 10-second interval.

**Acceptance:** Inject a CPU read failure after a valid sample and verify no new sample is fabricated. After more than three minutes without readings, old data is not presented as current history. Sleep/wake gaps and ordinary 10-second sampling remain distinguishable.

**Source:** Thermals/Monitor.swift:refresh/append; Thermals/Board/BoardComponents.swift:LampMeter; Thermals/Views/PopoverView.swift:footer

### UX-04: Bound popover height to the screen and reduce persistent settings overhead

P1. [GitHub #20](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/20).

**Evidence:** Fresh normal renders are 800x1638 pixels at renderer.scale=2, hence 400x819 points. Login error and update renders are 400x855 points. PopoverView uses the tallest of all three boards, four persistent preference rows, and no outer scrolling or screen-height constraint. Larger core counts increase intrinsic height. Live OS clipping was not observed because native automation timed out.

**Impact:** The intrinsic content exceeds an 800-point display before menu-bar margins. Settings and Quit may be hard to reach depending on host window sizing. Frequent glance interactions pay for rarely changed preferences.

**Proposed outcome:** Keep the stable footer within a bounded available height. Move rarely used preferences behind a Settings control, and prioritize the summary, history and diagnosis before detailed sensors/cores. Preserve the split-flap identity and fixed ordering of measured rows.

**Acceptance:** Verify all boards, update and login-error states on 1280x800 and 1440x900 logical desktops with relevant larger core counts. Every action remains reachable by pointer and keyboard, with stable placement within the available height.

**Source:** Thermals/Views/PopoverView.swift; CPUBoard.swift:CoreBoard; HeatBoard.swift:SensorBoard; RAMBoard.swift:AppMemoryBoard

### UX-05: Give unavailable and failed readings explicit states and recovery guidance

P2. [GitHub #21](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/21).

**Evidence:** Fresh no-sensors shows UNAVAILABLE while its chart says COLLECTING READINGS. The unavailable RAM render says SAMPLING indefinitely. ProcessBoard treats every empty process array as SAMPLING, including failed snapshots and empty valid results. No user-facing explanation differentiates warming up, unsupported access and failed reads. Fanless hardware and failure to open/read SMC both remove the fan section.

**Impact:** Users cannot tell whether to wait, retry, check compatibility or report a failure. A missing panel can look like a supported hardware conclusion.

**Proposed outcome:** Carry explicit initial/loading, available, unavailable, failed and stale source states as needed. Give brief contextual reasons and an appropriate recovery route. Do not invent a permission fix for a private sensor API failure.

**Acceptance:** Cold start, successful empty process sample, failed process read, unavailable sensor access, fanless hardware and SMC failure yield distinguishable and truthful UI states. Permanent failures do not continue to promise that sampling will finish.

**Source:** Thermals/Monitor.swift; Thermals/Views/HeatBoard.swift; CPUBoard.swift; RAMBoard.swift; Thermals/Board/BoardComponents.swift:LampMeter

### UX-06: Shorten and explain the wait for process attribution when opening CPU

P2. [GitHub #22](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/22).

**Evidence:** Changing samplesProcesses resets the sampler and clears the rows. Opening CPU obtains one baseline immediately; usable deltas wait for the next global timer, up to the selected 3/6/10-second interval. Closing or switching away resets this again. No process history is collected while the board is closed. This timing is inferred directly from Monitor and ProcessSampler, not measured in the live app.

**Impact:** A brief popover visit can end before the explanation appears. A short-lived heat spike may have ended before process attribution begins, so the app cannot reliably identify the earlier cause.

**Proposed outcome:** Take a prompt second baseline sample on CPU entry and explain its loading state. Provide a clear route from the heat overview to current CPU activity. Do not attribute a previous spike from current load alone; any background attribution tradeoff requires a separate energy-budget decision.

**Acceptance:** At the 10-second update interval, opening CPU can produce its first meaningful result promptly without waiting for the full interval. Label current activity honestly. Verify sampling ceases when no longer needed.

**Source:** Thermals/Monitor.swift:samplesProcesses/sampleProcesses; Thermals/Sensors/ProcessSampler.swift:sample; Thermals/Views/PopoverView.swift:sample

### UX-07: Keep thermal-pressure warnings consistent when temperature is low or unavailable

P1. [GitHub #23](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/23).

**Evidence:** HeatLevel.menuBar raises a temperature color for serious/critical pressure, but HeatBoard and CPUBoard use raw-temperature color. DESIGN.md states pressure raises the hero. With no temperature, ThermalsApp maps over nil and drops the pressure-raised level before constructing MenuBarReading; TEMP falls back to a thermometer and BOTH can show only load. The menu-bar accessibility text omits pressure.

**Impact:** The same temperature can communicate different urgency before and after opening. A critical OS thermal state can lose its menu-bar warning when the temperature sensor is unavailable.

**Proposed outcome:** Define one explicit warning contract for menu-bar and hero surfaces and keep pressure available independently of numeric temperature. Expose serious/critical state nonvisually and without requiring color alone. Reconcile the design documentation with the chosen behavior.

**Acceptance:** Verify 45C + serious, 45C + critical, and missing temperature + critical in TEMP and BOTH modes. The menu bar, header and accessible description consistently communicate the OS condition without inventing a temperature.

**Source:** Thermals/ThermalsApp.swift; Thermals/Views/HeatBoard.swift; CPUBoard.swift; Thermals/Sensors/HeatBand.swift; DESIGN.md

### UX-08: Prevent fixed-cell clipping from hiding names or changing numeric readings

P2. [GitHub #24](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/24).

**Evidence:** FlapText.characters uses uppercased().prefix(cells) for every string. Process/app names have 17 cells, sensor names 18, and there is no full-name hover/detail path in those rows. Numeric strings share that truncation. SMC accepts RPM through 20000 but FanBoard has four RPM cells, so 10000 would visually become 1000. The five-digit RPM example is a source-level boundary case, not observed hardware behavior.

**Impact:** Similar names can become indistinguishable; an overflowing number can look like a valid smaller measurement. Accessibility keeps the original text, so spoken and visible values can disagree.

**Proposed outcome:** Make truncation explicit for names and provide full-name access. Use a deliberate fitting/overflow policy for numeric readings that never silently changes magnitude. Preserve the fixed-cell visual language.

**Acceptance:** Check two long names sharing a prefix, long unknown sensors, 9999/10000/20000 RPM, and all numeric widths at supported boundaries. Visible and accessible values agree in meaning.

**Source:** Thermals/Board/Board.swift:FlapText; Thermals/Views/HeatBoard.swift:FanBoard; CPUBoard.swift; RAMBoard.swift; Thermals/Sensors/SMC.swift

### UX-09: Improve small critical-text contrast while preserving the board palette

P2. [GitHub #25](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/25).

**Evidence:** Burn-red E5383B has calculated sRGB contrast 3.62:1 against flap-top 242529 and 4.02:1 against flap-bottom 1B1C1F. Critical process names, statuses and pressure use small condensed flap text with a hinge cutting through glyphs. Neutral label color A7AAAF against frame 2A2D31 is 5.94:1. These are token-pair calculations, not full rendered accessibility certification.

**Impact:** The most urgent text can be harder to read than neutral text, especially for reduced contrast perception. Fixed geometry leaves little room for larger text.

**Proposed outcome:** Verify small-text contrast against the actual composited leaves and provide a brighter critical text treatment where necessary. Keep red for the warning signal and preserve readable values. Test increased contrast, display scaling, VoiceOver and keyboard focus before asserting accessibility coverage.

**Acceptance:** Small essential text reaches an agreed readable contrast target, using 4.5:1 as the normal-text benchmark. The hot/critical states retain their identity without sacrificing legibility. Record actual keyboard and assistive-technology results.

**Source:** Thermals/Board/Board.swift:darkHeatStops/FlapCell; Thermals/Board/BoardComponents.swift:ThermalState.ink/MemoryPressure.ink

### UX-10: Explain history scale and expose trend direction to assistive technology

P2. [GitHub #26](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/26).

**Evidence:** LampMeter rescales its eight rows to each window with a minimum span, but its header shows observed min/max rather than the scale bounds. For the cool RAM fixture the header is 47-53% while the fitted scale is at least 10 percentage points wide. There is no explicit NOW endpoint. The accessibility label contains only metric, period and min/max, so rising and falling series with the same extrema sound identical.

**Impact:** Similar-height charts can describe very different magnitudes; the range is easy to mistake for an axis. Nonvisual users cannot obtain the principal benefit of the history display: direction over time.

**Proposed outcome:** Keep the lamp bank but label observed min/max and time direction clearly. Expose a useful textual trend or accessible series, with no claims of future prediction. Coordinate gap semantics with the freshness issue.

**Acceptance:** Two equal-extrema series, one rising and one falling, are distinguishable to VoiceOver. A sighted user can identify the latest edge and understand whether the printed range is observed values or axis bounds.

**Source:** Thermals/Board/BoardComponents.swift:MeterScale/LampMeter

### UX-11: Represent launch-at-login approval pending separately from Off

P2. [GitHub #27](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/27).

**Evidence:** LaunchAtLogin.init(status:) maps every state except enabled to off. A register operation that yields requiresApproval opens System Settings but returns off and failed=false unless the API throws. PopoverView therefore has no pending explanation or persistent Settings route for that state. The existing failed-change row is useful and should remain.

**Impact:** After choosing ON, the app can still say OFF without explaining the outstanding OS approval. Users may repeat the action or assume their choice was lost.

**Proposed outcome:** Display a pending-approval explanation and a Settings route until the system reports enabled. Preserve truthful readback and explicit error handling.

**Acceptance:** Exercise notRegistered, requiresApproval, enabled and thrown-error states. Pending approval is distinct from a rejected operation and from intentional Off; reopening reflects the system's current state.

**Source:** Thermals/LoginItem.swift; Thermals/Views/PopoverView.swift

### UX-12: Expand UX fixtures to cover mixed states, compact screens and coherent CPU totals

P2. [GitHub #28](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/28).

**Evidence:** The existing 11 renders cover correlated cool/hot data, unavailable HEAT/RAM, Fahrenheit, update and login errors. They do not cover low temperature with critical pressure, high utilization with normal pressure, stale readings, long names or larger core layouts. PreviewMonitors.hot has a 96% headline but the 12 synthetic core usages average about 55%; these independently authored numbers do not prove calculation coherence. Snapshot settings inherit AppStorage for menu mode, so that control can vary with local preferences.

**Impact:** The current snapshots prove rendering, but can hide semantic conflicts and cannot certify numeric relationships, live scrolling, focus or screen-height handling.

**Proposed outcome:** Add deterministic mixed-state and boundary fixtures with coherent aggregate/core inputs where relevant, pin render preferences, and document a focused live keyboard/VoiceOver/small-screen checklist. Do not turn the audit's synthetic numbers into evidence of a production sampling bug.

**Acceptance:** Renders reproduce across user preferences and include the named adversarial states. Aggregate/core examples reconcile or explicitly model timing differences. Live checks cover behaviors the offscreen snapshotRendering path bypasses.

**Source:** Thermals/Views/PreviewMonitors.swift; ThermalsTests/PopoverSnapshotTests.swift; README.md

### UX-13: Reflect notification authorization in the Heat Alerts setting and provide recovery

P2. [GitHub #29](https://github.com/radkode/thermals/issues/29). Assessed at `c4657685a758e75893387130e710e152795c2404`.

**Evidence:** Concurrent heat-alert implementation adds an ON-by-default AppStorage preference. HeatAlerts requests notification authorization only when an alert triggers and returns silently if permission is denied. The popover shows ON based solely on that preference; no authorization-state readback or notification-settings recovery is rendered. Source inspection only; permission dialogs and delivery were not exercised.

**Impact:** A user can reasonably read ON as confirmation that heat notifications will arrive, even after macOS has denied permission. They have no visible explanation or recovery path in the app.

**Proposed outcome:** Distinguish the requested alert preference from delivery authorization. Show a concise blocked/pending state with a route to notification settings when needed; avoid forcing a first-run permission prompt merely to satisfy the indicator.

**Acceptance:** Verify notDetermined, authorized and denied notification states with the alert preference both on and off. The UI accurately communicates whether delivery is available, and a denied user can discover how to enable it.

**Source:** Thermals/HeatAlerts.swift; Thermals/Views/PopoverView.swift
