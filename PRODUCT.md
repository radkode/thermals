## Platform

ios

## Stack

SwiftUI menu bar app (`MenuBarExtra`, window style), project generated by XcodeGen from `project.yml`. Minimum macOS 14.

## Users

The owner, first: a developer who wants to see at a glance how hot their Mac is running while they work. Possibly Mac power users and developers later, once the tool has proven useful.

## Product Purpose

Thermals lives in the menu bar and shows how hot the Mac is right now, with CPU load and thermal pressure as the context around that number. Success: one glance at the menu bar answers "is my machine running hot," and one click explains why.

## Positioning

Temperature first. General stats monitors (Stats, iStat Menus, eul) give heat equal weight with memory, disk and network; Thermals leads with heat and treats everything else as supporting context.

## Operating Context

Glanced at during ordinary work: builds, video calls, gaming, heavy browser sessions. The popover is opened briefly and dismissed; it is never a destination window.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Distributed as a direct download, notarized, not sandboxed. This is what allows reading real sensor temperatures; the Mac App Store sandbox only exposes `ProcessInfo.thermalState`.
- Apple Silicon temperatures come from the IOKit HID event system, a private interface loaded at runtime. Sensor names differ by chip generation and the interface can change between macOS releases, so the app must degrade to "unavailable" rather than crash or guess.
- Intel Macs (SMC) are not supported yet.
- Built since launch: friendly sensor names, °C / °F, fan speeds (from the SMC; fanless Macs show no fan panel), launch at login.
- Undecided: power draw, GPU-specific readings.

## Product Principles

1. Heat is the headline; every other stat earns its place by explaining the heat.
2. Glanceable from the menu bar without opening anything.
3. Honest readings: show "unavailable" before showing a guessed number.
4. Feel like part of macOS, not a ported web dashboard.
